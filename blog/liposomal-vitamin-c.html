Dr. Mercola Liposomal Vitamin C Review: 1000 mg, Benefits & Uses

I looked at Dr. Mercola Liposomal Vitamin C because the 1,000 mg-per-serving format caught my attention immediately. The bottle contains 180 capsules, or 90 two-capsule servings, and the appeal is straightforward: a high-dose vitamin C supplement built around liposomal delivery. I also liked that the product is positioned around absorption and stomach comfort rather than simply pushing a bigger number.

One important distinction before I go further: this is an oral dietary supplement, not a vitamin C serum for the face. So when I talk about skin, I’m talking about vitamin C’s role in collagen production and antioxidant protection from the inside, not an instant brightening effect on the surface.

Is this product right for me?

For me, the first question would not be whether 1,000 mg sounds impressive. I’d ask whether I actually need a high-dose supplement and whether I tolerate vitamin C well. Adults generally need about 75 mg per day for women and 90 mg for men, while the adult upper limit from all sources is 2,000 mg daily. Food such as citrus fruits, bell peppers, and leafy greens can already provide plenty.

That makes this product more interesting for someone who specifically wants a concentrated supplement than for someone simply trying to cover basic nutrition. I’d also look at my total daily intake before adding another vitamin C product.

I would be more cautious if I had kidney disease, a history of kidney stones, or an iron-overload disorder such as hemochromatosis. High vitamin C intake can cause diarrhea, nausea, or stomach cramps, and it can increase iron absorption. Anyone pregnant, nursing, or taking prescription medicines should check with a healthcare professional before using a high-dose supplement.

Best Liposomal Vitamin C

I consider Dr. Mercola’s version a strong option if my priority is a capsule format, a 1,000 mg serving, and a brand that provides specific quality information. The current product information lists NSF Contents Certified, gluten-free, soy-free, and non-GMO positioning.

What I find more useful than the “best” label is the delivery format. Liposomal vitamin C uses phospholipid-based structures intended to help transport vitamin C through the digestive system. Recent research supports the basic idea that some liposomal formulations can increase vitamin C exposure in blood compared with conventional vitamin C, although results cannot automatically be applied to every brand or formulation.

A 2024 randomized crossover trial found higher plasma and leukocyte vitamin C exposure with a liposomal formulation than with standard vitamin C. A 2025 scoping review also found that most included studies showed higher bioavailability, while noting that formulations and doses varied considerably. So I see the liposomal advantage as promising rather than a reason to assume every liposomal product is automatically superior.

Key Benefits

The benefits I would realistically associate with this supplement come from vitamin C itself and, potentially, from its delivery format.

  • Better Absorption: Liposomal delivery may improve vitamin C exposure compared with conventional ascorbic acid, although the size of the advantage depends on the formulation.
  • Stomach Comfort: Dr. Mercola specifically positions the liposomal format as a way to reduce potential gastrointestinal distress. That does not mean everyone will tolerate 1,000 mg without symptoms.
  • Immune Support: Vitamin C contributes to normal immune function.
  • Collagen Production: Vitamin C is required for collagen biosynthesis, which matters for connective tissue and skin structure.
  • Antioxidant Protection: Vitamin C acts as an antioxidant and helps protect cells from oxidative damage.

What I would not promise is that taking this capsule will visibly tighten my skin, erase wrinkles, or prevent a cold. Those are much bigger claims than the evidence supports. Regular vitamin C supplementation may slightly shorten or lessen common-cold symptoms for some people, but it does not reliably prevent colds in most people.

liposomal vitamin c benefits

When I think about liposomal vitamin c benefits, I separate the nutrient benefits from the delivery benefits. Vitamin C is essential for collagen production, antioxidant function, iron absorption, and immune function. Those roles are well established.

The liposomal part is mainly about bioavailability. A conventional vitamin C supplement can be limited by intestinal absorption at higher doses. Liposomal delivery is designed to improve how the nutrient is handled by the digestive tract. That is why the format interests me more than simply choosing the highest milligram number.

For skin, I think of this as nutritional support rather than a replacement for topical skincare. Vitamin C contributes to collagen formation, but an oral supplement should not be expected to behave like a well-formulated topical vitamin C serum.

Key Ingredients

The main active ingredient is vitamin C in the form of ascorbic acid, with 1,000 mg provided per two-capsule serving according to the product information. The formulation also uses liposomal-delivery components, including phospholipid-based ingredients.

The ingredient point I would pay closest attention to is the actual Supplement Facts panel on the bottle I receive. Formulas can change, and retailer listings can sometimes lag behind current labels. I would check the current packaging rather than relying only on an old product description.

Liposomal Vitamin C Organic

I would not automatically call this an organic vitamin C supplement just because “organic” appears in a search for liposomal vitamin C. Organic certification is a separate claim that needs to be supported by the product label or certification.

For this Dr. Mercola product, the useful distinction is its liposomal format, capsule delivery, and listed quality attributes. If organic certification is a must-have for me, I would verify the exact certification on the current bottle before buying.

Liposomal Vitamin C 1000 mg

This is the version pictured here: 1,000 mg of vitamin C per two-capsule serving, with 180 capsules providing 90 servings. That works out to roughly a three-month supply when taken as directed.

I like the simplicity of the 1,000 mg format because it is easier to understand than products that push extremely large doses. Still, 1,000 mg is already a substantial amount compared with the daily requirement, so I would count vitamin C from other supplements too.

Liposomal Vitamin C 2200 mg

I would be careful with a 2200mg product simply because the number is higher. For adults, 2,000 mg per day is the established upper limit for vitamin C from all sources, so a 2,200 mg supplement would exceed that limit before food and other supplements are counted.

That does not make every 2,200 mg product inherently unsafe for every person, but it does make the dose something I would discuss with a healthcare professional rather than casually adding to my routine.

Liposomal Vitamin C 3000 mg

A 3000 mg liposomal vitamin C product is an even bigger step up. I would not choose it merely because it promises a stronger dose. High supplemental vitamin C can produce gastrointestinal effects such as diarrhea, nausea, and abdominal cramps.

For everyday wellness, more is not automatically better. I would first consider diet, my total supplemental intake, and the reason I want vitamin C before moving toward a dose this high.

liposomal vitamin c pills

Pills or capsules are the format I find easiest when I want consistency. There is no measuring spoon, no liquid to refrigerate, and no strong taste to deal with. With this product, the serving is two capsules.

That said, I would not confuse “easy to swallow” with “right for everyone.” Anyone who dislikes capsules may prefer liquid vitamin C, while someone sensitive to high-dose vitamin C may prefer a lower-dose product.

liposomal vitamin c liquid

Liquid liposomal vitamin C can be appealing if swallowing capsules is difficult or if I want more flexibility with dosing. The trade-off is that liquids can require refrigeration after opening, careful measuring, and attention to flavor and stability.

I would compare the actual vitamin C amount per serving rather than assuming liquid automatically absorbs better. The liposomal delivery system and formulation matter more than whether the supplement happens to be liquid.

liposomal vitamin c kids

I would not give this 1,000 mg adult product to a child simply because vitamin C is generally considered an important nutrient. Children's recommended amounts and upper limits are much lower and depend on age.

For example, the NIH lists upper limits of 400 mg for ages 1–3, 650 mg for ages 4–8, and 1,200 mg for ages 9–13. A child-specific product and pediatric guidance make much more sense.

Final Verdict

My overall take on Dr. Mercola Liposomal Vitamin C is that it makes the most sense for an adult who specifically wants a high-dose, capsule-based vitamin C supplement and is interested in liposomal delivery.

I also would not buy it solely for cosmetic promises. Vitamin C supports collagen production and antioxidant function, but I would still rely on sunscreen, a balanced diet, and evidence-based topical skincare for my broader beauty routine. If the price fits my budget and I have a clear reason to supplement, this is a formulation I would consider.
