How to Clean Makeup Brushes: A Simple, Sustainable Routine

Clean makeup brushes effortlessly at home. Use gentle shampoo in lukewarm water or rub bristles onto antibacterial soap to keep your brushes like new.

M
Maya Rodriguez
August 5, 2026
— Min Read

Okay, full disclosure: I am terrible about cleaning my makeup brushes. The internet says weekly. I'm lucky if it happens monthly. But when I do finally get around to it, I don't reach for a fancy brush shampoo or my regular hair shampoo anymore — I use plain, basic, ridiculously inexpensive castile soap, and it works better than anything else I've tried.

If you've been putting off cleaning your brushes because it feels like a whole production, this guide breaks it down into the fastest, easiest, most sustainable version I've found — plus a few natural swaps if you're out of your usual cleanser.

The Best Soap to Clean Makeup Brushes

I've bought the specialty brush cleaners before, and honestly, they never blew me away. What actually changed my brush-cleaning game was switching to unscented castile soap. It's a vegetable-based soap that started out in the Castile region of Spain, and the most well-known brand is Dr. Bronner's. It's gentle enough not to strip natural bristles, tough enough to break down foundation and SPF buildup, and one bottle lasts forever. I went with the unscented version since it's the most versatile — you can even add a drop of essential oil if you want a scent.

How to Clean Makeup Brushes at Home

This is the exact process I use, and it takes maybe five minutes once your brushes are actually in your hand (getting to that point is the hard part, no judgment).

Wet the Bristles First

Hold each brush upright and let water run over the bristles, or cup water in your hand and dip the brush in. I stick to cool-to-lukewarm water — hot water can loosen the glue that holds the bristles in place over time.

Work In the Soap

Add a few drops of castile soap to your palm or onto a textured brush-cleaning mat, then swirl the bristles through it in gentle circles. A cleaning mat makes this step so much faster because the little ridges pull product out of the bristles without you having to scrub. For really dirty brushes, rinse and repeat the soap step once or twice more.

Rinse Until the Water Runs Clear

Rinse the bristles and gently squeeze out the extra water — never twist or wring them. The color of the rinse water is basically a progress bar: keep repeating the soap-and-rinse cycle until it comes out clear instead of tinted.

The habit that actually matters more than the product you use is consistency, even if that means every two weeks instead of every week. How to Clean Makeup Brushes Fast

Some days a full wash isn't realistic, and that's fine — a fast spot-clean between deep cleans still keeps brushes usable and your skin happier. Spritz a bit of cleanser directly onto the bristles, swirl on a paper towel or clean cloth until the color lifts, then let the brush air dry for a few minutes before your next use.

How to Clean Makeup Brushes at Home With Micellar Water

Micellar water is my go-to for this quick version. The tiny cleansing molecules grab onto oil and pigment the same way they lift makeup off your face, so a few pumps onto a cloth is often enough to freshen up a foundation or concealer brush between real washes. It won't replace a deep clean, but it buys you a day or two.

How to Clean Makeup Brushes Naturally

If you'd rather skip anything synthetic, you have options beyond castile soap, too. The goal is the same either way — lift the oil and product without irritating your skin or damaging the bristles.

How to Clean Makeup Brushes at Home Without a Brush Cleaner

You genuinely don't need a dedicated brush cleanser at all. A gentle, unscented bar soap or a mild baby shampoo diluted in warm water will do most of the same work as castile soap. The main thing to avoid is anything with heavy fragrance or exfoliating beads, since both can irritate skin once they transfer back onto your face.

How to Clean Makeup Brushes With Vinegar

A splash of white vinegar in your rinse water is a solid natural disinfecting boost, especially for brushes that haven't been washed in a while. Mix about a tablespoon of vinegar into a cup of water, swirl the bristles through after your regular soap rinse, then rinse once more with plain water so no smell lingers. It's a nice occasional deep-clean step, but I wouldn't use it every single wash since vinegar is acidic and can be a little rough on delicate natural bristles over time.

How to Wash Makeup Sponges

Sponges and brushes aren't quite the same story. Because a sponge is porous, it holds onto way more product and bacteria per use than a brush does, so it honestly needs washing after almost every use, not just weekly. Wet the sponge, work a solid cleanser or a few drops of castile soap into it, and squeeze — don't scrub — until the water runs clear. A dedicated cleansing bar made for sponges works especially well here because it's gentle enough for daily use.

Pros & Cons of Cleaning Brushes With Castile Soap

It's become my default, but it's worth knowing where it falls short too.

Pros
  • Cheap, and one bottle lasts for months
  • Gentle on natural and synthetic bristles
  • Breaks down oil-based makeup effectively
  • Unscented option won't irritate sensitive skin
  • Sustainable, low-waste swap for single-use cleansers
  • Works on brushes, sponges, and cleaning mats
Cons
  • Needs a full rinse or it can feel slightly filmy
  • Won't disinfect as strongly as dish soap or vinegar
  • Bottle size can feel like overkill if you only own a few brushes
  • Doesn't speed up drying time on dense, bristle-heavy brushes

How Often to Clean Makeup Brushes

This really depends on how often you use them. If you're reaching for the same brushes daily, aim to wash them at least once a week, and more often if you have oily skin, since oil breaks down faster and builds up bacteria quicker. If you only use certain brushes occasionally, every four to six uses is a reasonable rule of thumb.

How to Dry Makeup Brushes

Drying is the step people skip, and it's actually the one most likely to cause problems if you rush it. Towel-dry the handles only — never the bristle head, since a towel can pick up germs and transfer them right back onto damp bristles.

Lay brushes flat over the edge of a counter or sink so the bristle head hangs off, not touching anything. Never stand a wet brush upright with the handle down, because water can seep into the ferrule and cause mold to grow inside the handle, which you obviously won't see until it's a problem.

Let brushes dry completely — usually a few hours, longer for bigger, denser ones. Gently press the brush head against the back of your hand; if it feels even slightly damp, give it more time before using it again.

The color of your rinse water is the best indicator you'll ever get. Keep rinsing until it runs clear, and your brushes are actually clean — not just rinsed.

A quick note on hygiene beyond the brushes themselves: Allure's brush-hygiene reporting has repeatedly found that unwashed brushes can carry surprising amounts of bacteria, which is a good reminder that this isn't just an aesthetic habit — it affects your skin too.

Our Final Verdict

You don't need a drawer full of specialty products to keep your brushes clean. One bottle of unscented castile soap, five minutes, and a habit you can actually stick to will get you further than any fancy brush shampoo. Whether you wash weekly, monthly, or somewhere in between (no judgment here), the wet-soap-rinse-dry method is the one thing worth remembering.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the best thing to clean makeup brushes with? ▼
A gentle, unscented castile soap is one of the best options because it breaks down oil and makeup residue without stripping or drying out natural bristles. It's affordable, works on both natural and synthetic brushes, and doesn't require any special brush-cleaning gadget.
Can you use Dawn dish soap to clean your makeup brushes? ▼
You can, since dish soap is very effective at cutting through oil, but it's also stronger than most brushes need on a regular basis. It works best as an occasional deep-clean option rather than your weekly go-to, especially for softer synthetic bristles.
How do you deep clean your makeup brushes? ▼
A deep clean uses the same wet, soap, rinse, dry process, but you repeat the soap step two or three times until the water runs completely clear and you gently work the cleanser into the base of the bristles, not just the tips, where product tends to build up.
How do professional makeup artists clean their brushes? ▼
Many pros use a gentle liquid cleanser and a textured silicone mat or glove to work product out of the bristles quickly between clients, then let brushes air dry flat. The tools are professional-grade, but the underlying method is the same wet-soap-rinse-dry approach anyone can do at home.
What kills bacteria on makeup brushes? ▼
Regular washing with soap and water is what actually removes the bacteria, oil, and dead skin cells that build up on brushes with everyday use. Letting brushes dry completely between uses also matters, since bacteria multiplies fastest in damp bristles.
How to clean makeup brushes for first time use? ▼
Brand-new brushes should be washed before their first use to remove manufacturing residue and loose fibers. A quick wash with a gentle soap, a good rinse, and a full dry lying flat is all it takes to get them ready.
Should I soak my makeup brushes in water? ▼
Brief soaking is fine, but you should never fully submerge the brush head for an extended period or let water reach the ferrule, the metal part that holds the bristles to the handle, since that can loosen the glue over time.
How to clean makeup brushes real techniques? ▼
The most reliable technique is simple: wet the bristles, work in a gentle cleanser using circular motions in your palm or on a textured mat, rinse until the water runs clear, then reshape and dry the brush flat.
Can I wash my makeup brushes with hand soap? ▼
Plain, gentle hand soap can work in a pinch, though it's not as effective at breaking down oil-based makeup as a castile soap or dedicated brush cleanser. It's a reasonable backup option, just not the best everyday choice.
What's the best homemade brush cleaner? ▼
A simple mix of a few drops of unscented castile soap in warm water makes an effective, budget-friendly homemade cleaner. Some people add a splash of white vinegar to the rinse water for extra disinfecting power on very dirty brushes.
How to get makeup brushes fluffy again? ▼
After washing, gently reshape the bristles with your fingers while they're still damp, then lay the brush flat to dry so it keeps its original shape instead of drying bent or matted.
