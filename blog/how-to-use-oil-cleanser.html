How to Use Oil Cleanser: Easy Step-by-Step Guide In 2026

Learn how to use oil cleanser the right way — on face and body, Korean-style, for blackheads, sebaceous filaments, and everyday skin health.

S
Dr. Sarah Collins
August 2, 2026
— Min Read

Let's be real — washing your face with oil sounds like the last thing your skin needs, especially if you're already dealing with shine or breakouts. But oil cleansing is one of those skincare tricks that actually works once you get the hang of it. If you've been Googling how to use oil cleanser without turning your bathroom sink into a grease trap, you're in the right place.

This guide breaks down everything: what oil cleansers actually do, who they're for, and the exact steps to use one properly, whether you're targeting blackheads, sebaceous filaments, or just want a gentler nighttime routine.

What is an Oil Cleanser

An oil cleanser is exactly what it sounds like — a face wash built on an oil base instead of water. Most foaming cleansers, gels, and creams you're used to are water-based, which means they can only do so much when it comes to lifting off makeup, sunscreen, and the natural oil your skin produces. An oil-based formula works differently: it grabs onto that oily buildup because oil is naturally drawn to oil. Once you add water and rinse, all of it — the product, the grime, the leftover SPF — washes straight down the drain.

Who Should Use an Oil Cleanser

Here's the good news: oil cleansers genuinely work for pretty much every skin type, not just dry skin.

  • Dry skin gets an extra dose of moisture from the oil itself
  • Oily skin benefits because balanced hydration means less overproduction of sebum
  • Anyone who wears SPF daily (which should be everyone) gets a proper, thorough cleanse
  • Makeup wearers get a much cleaner finish without harsh scrubbing
Editor's Pick

A lot of people don't realize that "oily" skin is often just dehydrated skin overcompensating. Your skin produces extra sebum to fight dryness, and a stripping cleanser only makes that cycle worse. An oil cleanser doesn't pull moisture out the way a lot of regular face washes do, so your skin has less reason to go into overdrive. How Does Oil Cleanser Work

The science behind it is honestly pretty simple, and it comes down to a few things happening at once.

Oil Attracts Oil and Breaks Down Buildup

Your pores get clogged with all kinds of stuff over the course of a day — makeup, sweat, SPF, dead skin, and your own natural oil. The oil in your cleanser latches onto every bit of that so it can be washed away instead of sitting in your pores.

Emulsifiers Help It Rinse Clean

Emulsifiers are the ingredient doing the behind-the-scenes work. They allow oil and water to mix, which is exactly why adding water at the end of your cleanse makes everything rinse off smoothly instead of leaving a greasy film.

It Protects Your Skin's Moisture Barrier

Unlike a lot of regular cleansers that leave your skin feeling tight and stripped, an oil-based formula doesn't pull moisture out of your skin. That means your barrier stays balanced instead of getting damaged over time.

It Sets You Up for a Proper Double Cleanse

Using an oil cleanser as step one lets a water-based cleanser actually do its job as step two — cleaning your skin instead of fighting through a layer of makeup and SPF first. It's a small change that makes a noticeably bigger difference.

Oil Cleansing Benefits

What You Get
  • Removes makeup and sunscreen without a fight
  • Clears out pores far more thoroughly than water-based washes
  • Keeps skin hydrated instead of stripped
  • Supports a stronger, healthier skin barrier
  • Helps balance excess oil production over time
  • Leaves skin looking smoother and more even
Things to Watch For
  • The wrong oil can clog pores on very acne-prone skin
  • Skipping the double cleanse can leave residue behind
  • Some formulas feel too heavy or fragrant for sensitive skin
  • Can be tricky around false lashes if not applied carefully

How to Use Cleansing Oil

Here's the part everyone actually wants — the step-by-step on how to use oil cleanser without making a mess of your bathroom sink.

  1. Start with completely dry hands and a dry face — no damp towel, no splash of water first.
  2. Pump 1–2 pumps of oil cleanser into your palm and rub your hands together to warm it up.
  3. Massage it gently into your skin using light, circular motions — never tug or pull.
  4. Keep massaging for at least 30 seconds, covering your hairline, nose, and eye area.
  5. Add a splash of water to your hands and continue massaging so the product starts to emulsify.
  6. Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water until your skin feels clean, not greasy or filmy.
  7. Follow up with a water-based cleanser if you're double cleansing tonight.
  8. Pat your face dry and move on to the rest of your skincare routine.

A Quick Note on Eye Makeup

Oil cleanser is genuinely one of the easiest ways to melt off stubborn eye makeup, including waterproof mascara. Just be careful if you wear false lashes regularly — oil can loosen the lash glue faster than you'd expect, so check with your lash tech first.

How to Use Oil Cleanser on Face

On the face specifically, less is more. Focus the bulk of the product on your T-zone and any spot where you wore makeup or SPF, then work outward toward your cheeks and jawline with a lighter touch. Dry patches, like your cheeks in colder months, only need a gentle pass since they don't have as much buildup to break down. Give your jawline and hairline a little extra attention too — they're the spots people tend to rush past, and they're usually where product residue lingers the longest.

How to Use Oil Cleanser Korean Style

The Korean beauty world basically popularized oil cleansing as we know it today, and their method leans hard into the double cleanse. The idea is simple: cleansing oil or balm goes on first to dissolve makeup, SPF, and sebum, followed immediately by a low-pH, water-based cleanser — often a foaming or gel formula — to finish the job and prep skin for the rest of a multi-step routine.

The K-beauty approach also tends to favor longer massage times, sometimes a full minute or two, treating the cleanse itself as a mini facial massage rather than a rushed step you blow through.

How to Use Oil Cleanser to Remove Blackheads

Oil cleansing won't erase blackheads overnight, but consistent use genuinely helps loosen the oxidized gunk sitting in your pores. Massage a little longer than usual directly over blackhead-prone areas like your nose and chin — 60 seconds instead of 30 — using slow, circular pressure rather than scrubbing.

Warm water helps soften pores beforehand, so consider steaming your face with a warm washcloth for a minute before you start. Pair this with a gentle exfoliating acid a few times a week, and you'll likely notice pores looking clearer over a few weeks, not overnight.

How to Use Cleansing Oil on Body

Oil cleansing isn't just for your face. On the body, it works especially well on rough patches like elbows, knees, and shins, or for anyone dealing with dry, flaky winter skin. Apply it in the shower on dry skin before you turn the water on, massaging in slow circles over particularly dry areas. Once the water's running, let it emulsify and rinse away just like you would on your face. It's also a nice gentle option for removing self-tanner or leftover body lotion buildup without over-scrubbing your skin.

The biggest mistake people make with body oil cleansing is treating it like a regular body wash and rushing through it — the massage time is what actually makes it effective.

Oil Cleansing Method Ruined My Skin

If oil cleansing left you breaking out or looking worse, you're not alone, and it usually comes down to one of a few fixable issues rather than the method itself being bad.

  • Wrong oil for your skin type — heavier, more comedogenic oils like coconut oil can clog acne-prone pores.
  • Skipping the second cleanse — leaving oil residue on your skin overnight can trap bacteria and debris.
  • Not rinsing long enough — a rushed rinse leaves a film behind that attracts more grime.
  • Mistaking purging for breakouts — some people experience a short adjustment period as skin detoxes; this typically settles within a few weeks.

If breakouts continue past a few weeks, it's worth switching to a lighter, non-comedogenic oil or checking in with a dermatologist to rule out an underlying skin condition.

How to Use Oil Cleanser to Remove Sebaceous Filaments

Sebaceous filaments often get mistaken for blackheads, but they're actually a normal part of your skin's oil-regulating system, appearing as tiny gray or tan dots mostly around the nose. Oil cleansing helps keep them looking less noticeable by regularly clearing out the sebum that fills them back in.

Massage gently over the nose and surrounding areas for a bit longer than the rest of your face, and consider following up with a gentle konjac sponge for extra, non-abrasive exfoliation. Just know upfront that sebaceous filaments can't be permanently removed — they'll always be there, but consistent oil cleansing keeps them far less visible.

Do You Use Oil Cleanser Everyday

For most people, once a day is the sweet spot — ideally in the evening, when there's actual makeup, SPF, and grime to break down. Using it twice a day isn't necessary for most skin types and can occasionally feel like overkill on already-dry skin. If your skin is on the drier side or you don't wear much makeup, using it in the morning instead of at night works just as well. The key is consistency, not frequency — a nightly habit will do far more for your skin than an occasional deep-clean session.

Best Oil Cleansers

There are plenty of clean, well-formulated oil cleansers out there, and which one works best for you often comes down to scent and texture preference more than anything else. Some formulas feel too fragrant even when they're marketed as clean, and others are thick enough that massaging them in feels like a workout.

A good starting formula should glide on easily, emulsify quickly with water, and leave zero greasy residue behind — that's the standard worth holding every cleansing oil to. Browse our skincare category for options that fit different skin types and budgets.

Our Final Verdict

Oil cleansing might sound counterintuitive, but once you understand how to use oil cleanser correctly, it's genuinely one of the gentlest, most effective ways to get your skin properly clean. Whether you're using it on your face, your body, or as the first step of a K-beauty-style double cleanse, the technique matters just as much as the product — dry hands, a slow massage, and a thorough rinse.

Give it a few weeks before judging the results. Skin needs time to adjust to any new routine, and the payoff — a balanced, properly hydrated barrier — is worth the short learning curve.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do you use oil cleanser before or after face wash? ▼
Oil cleanser always goes first. It's step one of a double cleanse, meant to break down makeup, SPF, and sebum before your regular face wash goes in and finishes the job on clean, product-free skin.
Should you use oil cleanser on wet or dry skin? ▼
Start on completely dry skin with dry hands. Oil binds to the oil-based buildup on your face far better this way. Add water only toward the end, once you're ready to emulsify and rinse everything away.
How many times a day do I use an oil cleanser? ▼
Once a day is plenty for most people, ideally at night to melt away the day's makeup, SPF, and grime. Some people with very dry or makeup-free skin prefer using it in the morning instead, and that works too.
What are the steps to use an oil cleanser properly? ▼
Apply 1-2 pumps to dry hands and a dry face, massage in circular motions for 30 seconds, add water to emulsify, then rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water before your next cleanser.
What exactly does oil cleanser do? ▼
It uses oil to attract and dissolve the oil-based buildup on your skin, like makeup, sunscreen, and excess sebum, then rinses it away with water thanks to emulsifiers, all without stripping your skin's natural moisture.
