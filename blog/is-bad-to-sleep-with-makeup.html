Is It Bad to Sleep With Makeup? Here’s What Happens

Is it bad to sleep with makeup? Yes. Learn how leaving makeup on while you sleep can clog pores, trigger acne, cause eye irritation and premature aging.

M
Maya Rodriguez
August 10, 2026
— Min Read

After a long day, removing makeup can feel like one more task you would rather skip. Maybe you are exhausted, came home late, or simply think that sleeping in makeup for one night will not make much difference.

But is it bad to sleep with makeup? In most cases, yes. Leaving foundation, concealer, powder, blush, or other face makeup on overnight can contribute to clogged pores, breakouts and skin irritation. Even makeup labeled non-comedogenic can contribute to acne when it is left on while you sleep.

Makeup is designed to stay on your skin for hours, but that does not mean it should remain there overnight. During the day, your skin is exposed to sweat, oil, dirt and environmental particles. When makeup is left on top of all of this while you sleep, your skin does not get the opportunity to start the night with a clean surface.

The good news is that preventing these problems does not require an expensive or complicated routine. Removing makeup gently before bed is one of the simplest skincare habits you can develop — and if you'd rather browse gentle formulas while you read, our makeup category rounds up options worth adding to your routine.

Below, we will look at the main reasons dermatologists recommend removing makeup before bedtime, including what can happen to your pores, eyes, eyelashes and overall skin appearance.

10 Reasons Why Sleeping in Makeup Is Bad for Your Skin

Sleeping in makeup is not necessarily disastrous after a single night, but regularly doing it can work against healthy-looking skin. Here are 10 important reasons to make makeup removal part of your bedtime routine.

1. It Can Clog Your Pores

One of the most common concerns associated with sleeping in makeup is clogged pores.

Throughout the day, your skin naturally produces oil and sheds dead skin cells. Sweat and environmental debris can also collect on the surface. Makeup adds another layer to the skin.

When makeup is not removed before bed, the combination of makeup, oil, sweat and dead skin cells can contribute to pore congestion.

Clogged pores may appear as blackheads, whiteheads or small bumps. For people who already have oily or acne-prone skin, regularly sleeping in makeup may make breakouts more likely.

This is one reason choosing non-comedogenic makeup can be helpful, although even non-comedogenic products should still be removed before sleeping.

2. It Can Cause Skin Irritation

Another reason to remove makeup before bed is that prolonged contact with cosmetic ingredients may irritate sensitive skin.

Different makeup products contain pigments, preservatives, fragrances and other ingredients. Most people can use these products without a problem, but sensitive skin may react to certain ingredients.

You may notice:

  • Redness
  • Itching
  • Dryness
  • Burning or stinging
  • Flaking
  • Small irritated bumps

If you repeatedly develop irritation after using a particular product, it may be better to stop using it rather than simply continuing and hoping your skin adjusts.

3. It May Make Your Skin Look Older Over Time

One of the most frequently discussed concerns about sleeping in makeup is premature-looking skin aging.

However, it is important to avoid exaggerated claims. There is no reliable formula that says sleeping in makeup for one night adds a particular number of years to your face.

Skin aging is influenced by many factors, including genetics, sun exposure, environmental factors and lifestyle.

Still, regularly going to bed without cleansing is an avoidable skincare habit. Removing makeup allows you to properly cleanse your skin and follow your nighttime skincare routine.

4. It Can Cause Problems Around the Eyes

Eye makeup deserves extra attention because products such as mascara, eyeliner and eyeshadow are used very close to the eyes.

Leaving eye makeup on overnight can increase the possibility of irritation, especially if the product becomes dry or transfers around the eye area.

The skin around the eyes is also particularly delicate. Rubbing aggressively to remove old makeup the following morning can create another problem by irritating the skin.

For this reason, eye makeup should be removed gently before going to bed.

5. It Can Contribute to Acne Flare-Ups

Acne is influenced by several factors, including oil production, dead skin cells, hormones and clogged hair follicles.

Sleeping in makeup can be one factor that contributes to congestion, particularly for people who are already prone to acne.

This does not mean every person who sleeps in makeup will wake up with acne. However, if breakouts are a concern, removing makeup before bed is a simple habit that can help reduce avoidable triggers.

6. It Can Make Your Skin-Care Routine More Difficult

Your nighttime skincare routine works best when you begin with clean skin.

If foundation, concealer, powder or other makeup remains on your face, applying moisturizer or skincare treatments over it means you are not starting with a properly cleansed surface.

You do not need a complicated nighttime routine. For many people, the basics are enough:

Remove makeup → cleanse gently → moisturize.

If you use specific acne or prescription treatments, follow the instructions provided by your dermatologist or healthcare professional.

7. It May Contribute to an Uneven-Looking Complexion

Repeated irritation and inflammation can affect how your skin looks.

For example, if sleeping in makeup contributes to repeated breakouts, the inflammation associated with those breakouts may eventually leave temporary discoloration in some people.

This does not mean makeup automatically causes uneven skin tone. Rather, repeated irritation, acne and inappropriate product use can contribute to changes in the appearance of the skin.

If a product consistently causes redness or irritation, consider replacing it with a gentler alternative. If dark patches or uneven tone linger after a flare-up settles, our best vitamin C serum for hyperpigmentation guide can help fade them once the skin has calmed down.

8. It Can Affect Your Eyelashes

Mascara is designed to coat the eyelashes, but it should not be left on indefinitely.

When mascara dries overnight, removing it later may require more rubbing than normal. Excessive rubbing around the eyes can be irritating and may contribute to unnecessary stress on the eyelashes.

Rather than worrying about one accidental night, focus on making gentle mascara removal part of your regular routine.

9. It May Increase the Risk of Eye or Skin Problems

Cosmetic products can become contaminated through repeated use, especially when hygiene is poor.

Using old makeup, sharing eye makeup or applying products with unclean hands can increase the risk of contamination.

This is particularly important with products used around the eyes.

Regularly cleaning applicable makeup tools and replacing products according to the manufacturer's recommendations are important parts of cosmetic hygiene. Speaking of tools, our how to clean makeup brushes guide covers the basics of keeping your applicators sanitary.

10. It Prevents You From Starting the Night With Clean Skin

Perhaps the simplest reason to remove makeup is that your skin accumulates things throughout the day.

Oil, sweat, makeup and environmental debris do not need to stay on your face while you sleep.

A clean face gives you a better starting point for your nighttime skincare routine and helps you avoid making makeup removal tomorrow morning a much bigger task.

Sleeping With Makeup on for One Night

So, what happens if you accidentally fall asleep with makeup on for one night?

First, don't panic.

Sleeping with makeup on for one night is unlikely to cause permanent damage to your skin. You may notice that your skin looks oilier, duller or more congested the next morning, but that does not mean you have permanently harmed it.

The most sensible response is to gently remove the makeup when you wake up.

Avoid trying to "fix" the situation by scrubbing your face aggressively or applying multiple strong exfoliating products. Over-treating your skin can cause irritation.

Instead:

  1. Remove your makeup gently.
  2. Cleanse your face with a suitable cleanser.
  3. Rinse with lukewarm water.
  4. Apply a moisturizer if your skin needs it.
  5. Return to your normal skincare routine.

The real concern is not an occasional accident. It is repeatedly making sleeping in makeup part of your routine.

Top Reasons to Remove Your Makeup Before Bed

Removing makeup before bed has several practical benefits.

First, it helps remove products and debris that accumulated throughout the day. Second, it reduces the amount of makeup sitting on your skin overnight. Third, it gives you a clean surface for your nighttime skincare routine.

For acne-prone skin, choosing non-comedogenic products and removing them properly is especially important.

Most importantly, makeup removal does not have to take ten minutes. If you are exhausted, even a simple and gentle routine is better than routinely going to sleep with a full face of makeup.

Not Removing Your Makeup Will Clog Your Pores

The relationship between makeup and clogged pores is worth understanding.

A pore can become congested when oil and dead skin cells accumulate within the follicle. Some cosmetic products may add to that buildup, particularly when they are left on for prolonged periods.

This is why people who are prone to blackheads, whiteheads or acne may benefit from being especially consistent about makeup removal.

However, clogged pores are not caused by makeup alone. Hormonal changes, genetics, skin type and other skincare habits also play important roles.

Sleeping in Makeup Can Cause Skin Irritation

If you have sensitive skin, sleeping in makeup may be particularly uncomfortable.

Cosmetic ingredients that are perfectly fine for one person may irritate another person's skin. Leaving products on for an extended period can give the skin more opportunity to react.

If you wake up with redness, itching or burning after sleeping in makeup, gently cleanse your skin and keep your routine simple.

If the reaction is severe, persistent or repeatedly occurs with a particular cosmetic product, consider speaking with a dermatologist or other healthcare professional.

Leaving Makeup on While Sleeping Can Age Your Skin

The phrase "makeup ages your skin" is often used online, but it needs some context.

Makeup itself is not automatically a cause of premature aging. However, regularly sleeping without cleansing your skin can contribute to irritation and poor skincare habits.

There are also much more established factors involved in visible skin aging, particularly cumulative sun exposure.

Therefore, rather than worrying about one night of sleeping in makeup, focus on consistent habits such as cleansing, moisturizing and protecting your skin from excessive sun exposure. A hydrating step also helps — our best hydrating face mist picks are a nice addition after cleansing.

How Much Does Sleeping in Makeup Age Your Skin?

There is no scientifically established number of years that sleeping in makeup adds to someone's skin age.

You may see online claims suggesting that one night of sleeping in makeup adds a specific number of years to your appearance. These claims should be viewed skeptically because skin aging cannot be calculated that way.

Your skin changes because of many interacting factors.

If you occasionally forget to remove makeup, you do not need to worry that you have permanently aged your skin. Instead, make makeup removal a consistent habit going forward.

Makeup Left on for Extended Periods of Time Can Cause Infections

Cosmetic products can become contaminated, particularly when they are old, shared between people or handled with unclean hands.

The risk is especially relevant around the eyes because eye makeup comes into close contact with sensitive tissues.

Leaving makeup on for extended periods does not automatically mean you will develop an infection. However, good cosmetic hygiene is important.

Avoid sharing mascara or eyeliner, keep makeup applicators clean and replace products according to their recommended usage period.

If you develop significant eye redness, swelling, discharge or persistent discomfort, seek medical advice rather than continuing to use the suspected product.

Is It Bad to Sleep With Eye Makeup On?

Yes, it is better not to sleep with eye makeup on.

Mascara, eyeliner and eyeshadow can transfer during sleep and may irritate the surrounding skin or eyes. Removing eye makeup before bed also means you do not have to aggressively rub the area the following morning.

When removing eye makeup, be gentle. Allow your makeup remover to loosen the product instead of repeatedly rubbing the eyelids.

If your eyes become persistently red, swollen or uncomfortable, stop using the suspected cosmetic and seek professional medical advice.

Makeup Can Cause Reduced Skin Oxygenation

You may have heard that makeup "stops your skin from breathing."

This is a popular beauty phrase, but it is not scientifically precise.

Your skin does not breathe like your lungs. Oxygen reaches living skin cells primarily through the bloodstream rather than simply passing from the air through makeup.

Therefore, it is more accurate to discuss the real concerns associated with leaving makeup on for too long: clogged pores, irritation, breakouts and poor cleansing habits.

This distinction matters because good skincare advice should be based on what actually happens in the skin rather than repeating popular beauty myths. According to the American Academy of Dermatology, sleeping in makeup is one of the everyday habits that can quietly worsen acne, regardless of whether the formula is labeled non-comedogenic.

You Can Have an Allergic Reaction

Some people can experience an allergic or irritant reaction to cosmetic products.

A reaction may cause symptoms such as:

  • Itching
  • Redness
  • Burning
  • Swelling
  • Rash
  • Dry or flaky skin

Not every reaction is a true allergy. Some are caused by irritation or sensitivity to an ingredient.

If a particular makeup product repeatedly causes symptoms, stop using it and consider discussing the reaction with a dermatologist.

Patch testing may also be recommended in some situations to help identify potential triggers.

Makeup Left on Overnight Can Cause Acne Flare-Ups

If you already have acne-prone skin, sleeping in makeup may make your skincare routine more difficult.

Makeup can mix with oil, sweat and dead skin cells on the skin's surface. If the combination contributes to pore congestion, you may notice more bumps or breakouts.

That does not mean makeup is the sole cause of acne.

Acne is a complex condition influenced by hormones, genetics, oil production and other factors. Still, removing makeup before bed is an easy habit that may help reduce unnecessary congestion.

Does Sleeping With Makeup on Cause Acne?

Yes, It can contribute to breakouts, but it is not the only cause of acne.

People with acne-prone skin should pay attention to how their skin responds to different products. Look for makeup labeled non-comedogenic or products designed specifically for acne-prone skin.

More importantly, remove makeup before sleeping.

If acne continues despite good skincare habits, it may require a more targeted treatment plan. Persistent or severe acne is best discussed with a dermatologist rather than treated by continuously adding new products.

Makeup Left on for Extended Periods of Time Can Cause Uneven Skin Tone

Repeated breakouts and inflammation can sometimes leave temporary discoloration behind.

For some people, this can make the complexion appear less even.

Again, makeup is not automatically responsible for uneven skin tone. Many factors — including acne, sun exposure and inflammation — can affect pigmentation.

The best approach is to avoid unnecessary irritation and use a skincare routine appropriate for your skin.

Weakened Eyelashes

Your eyelashes are delicate, and the area around your eyes should be treated gently.

Sleeping with mascara can make the product dry and more difficult to remove. If you then rub your eyelashes aggressively, you may cause unnecessary mechanical stress.

The solution is simple: remove mascara gently every night.

You should also avoid sharing mascara and replace eye products according to the manufacturer's recommendations.

Is It Bad to Sleep With Mascara on for One Night?

No, If you accidentally sleep with mascara on for one night, it is unlikely to cause permanent damage.

However, that does not make it a good habit. When you wake up, use a suitable eye-makeup remover and gently loosen the mascara. Avoid pulling or rubbing your eyelashes.

If you experience eye pain, significant swelling, persistent redness or vision changes, seek medical attention rather than assuming the problem is simply leftover mascara.

How Many Times a Week Should I Wear Mascara?

There is no universal rule saying that mascara should only be worn a certain number of times per week.

You can wear mascara regularly if your eyes tolerate it and you use the product safely.

The more important habits are:

  • Remove mascara before sleeping.
  • Do not share mascara.
  • Avoid using products that cause irritation.
  • Keep the applicator clean.
  • Replace mascara according to the manufacturer's recommended period.
  • Stop using a product if it causes repeated eye irritation.

If your eyes are sensitive, you may need to be more selective about the products you use.

It Will Make Skin Care More Difficult

Sleeping in makeup can make your skincare routine unnecessarily complicated.

Instead of beginning your nighttime routine with clean skin, you wake up with yesterday's makeup still on your face.

You may then feel tempted to wash your face repeatedly or use harsh exfoliating products to remove everything.

That can create another problem: irritation.

A better approach is prevention. Remove your makeup before bed so your skin is already clean when you begin your nighttime routine. If your skin also runs on the more mature or easily irritated side, our best cleansers for mature skin roundup is worth a look too.

Woman Sleeping With Makeup on for 20 Years

You may have come across stories online about a woman sleeping with makeup on for 20 years and claims that this supposedly demonstrates exactly what makeup does to the skin.

Individual stories can be interesting, but they should not be treated as scientific evidence.

A person's skin after 20 years is affected by countless variables, including genetics, age, sun exposure, lifestyle, hormones, skincare habits and the specific products they used.

Therefore, it is impossible to say that a particular appearance was caused entirely by sleeping in makeup.

The more useful lesson is simply that consistently removing makeup is a healthy and easy skincare habit.

Permanent Makeup Alternatives

If the main reason you sleep in makeup is convenience, you may wonder about permanent makeup.

Permanent makeup is different from conventional cosmetics because pigment is placed into the skin. Depending on the procedure, results may last for a long time and require professional treatment and aftercare.

It should therefore not be viewed as a simple substitute for washing your face at night.

If you are considering permanent makeup, research the procedure carefully and discuss its potential benefits, limitations and risks with a qualified professional.

For most people, a much simpler alternative is to create a quick nighttime routine that takes only a few minutes.

If tiredness is the reason you skip makeup removal, make the process easier.

Keep your cleanser or makeup remover somewhere convenient. Remove your makeup before getting too comfortable in bed rather than waiting until you are already exhausted.

You can also simplify your routine on busy nights.

You do not need ten different products. A gentle makeup-removal step followed by cleansing and moisturizing may be enough for a basic routine. Final Verdict: Is It Bad to Sleep With Makeup?

So, is it bad to sleep with makeup? Yes — especially when it becomes a regular habit.

One accidental night does not mean that you have permanently damaged your skin. However, there is little benefit to leaving makeup on overnight, while there are several avoidable reasons to remove it.

Sleeping in makeup can contribute to clogged pores, acne flare-ups, skin irritation and problems around the eyes. It can also make your nighttime skincare routine more difficult.

The solution is surprisingly simple:

Remove your makeup. Cleanse gently. Moisturize if needed. Go to bed with clean skin.
